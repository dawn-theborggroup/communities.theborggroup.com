The Quick Answer

What is Pelican Landing?

Pelican Landing is a gated, master-planned community of 2,365 acres in Bonita Springs, stretching west of U.S. 41 to the waters of Estero Bay, with Spring Creek — a state-designated Outstanding Florida Water — running along its southern edge. Development began with Pelican's Nest Golf Club in 1985, the first residents arrived in 1990, and today roughly 3,300 residences sit among mature oaks, preserves, and lakes across dozens of distinct neighborhoods.

What sets Pelican Landing apart isn't just the nature — it's what your association fees include. The signature amenity is a private 34-acre beach island on the Gulf, reached only by the community's own boat shuttle. Add the tennis and pickleball center, sailing center, kayak park, fitness center, and marina access, and you have one of the richest included-amenity packages in Southwest Florida — with golf as an optional layer on top, never a requirement.

The Island

What's the private beach island actually like?

Picture this: you park at the community marina on Estero Bay, step onto a Pelican Landing shuttle boat, and cruise to the northern tip of Big Hickory Island — where 34 acres of Gulf beachfront belong to your community alone. The Beach Park is staffed year-round and genuinely equipped: an elevated beach house with open decks, restrooms and showers, two open-air picnic pavilions plus a large screened pavilion, beach bocce, and a supply of chairs and umbrellas waiting for you.

In a region where public beach parking disappears by mid-morning in season, a boat-access-only private beach is the amenity money usually can't buy — only two communities in the area share an island experience like this, and Pelican Landing's is included with residency. It's the single feature that makes longtime residents say they could never live anywhere else.

Golf & Amenities

What about golf, tennis, and life on the water?

The Nest — Pelican's Nest Golf Club — anchors the community with two 18-hole Tom Fazio Signature courses winding along Spring Creek and Estero Bay, run as a member-owned club with an updated 50,000-square-foot clubhouse and a busy year-round calendar. Membership is optional, which keeps the community welcoming for golfers and non-golfers alike.

The association amenities carry the rest of the lifestyle: a tennis center with twelve Har-Tru courts, six pickleball courts, bocce, a community center with a fully equipped fitness facility, and — this is where Pelican Landing shines — real water access: a sailing center, a kayak and canoe park on Spring Creek, leasable boat slips at the community marina and Spring Creek dock, and a boat ramp for owners who store elsewhere.

Homes & Neighborhoods

What kinds of homes are in Pelican Landing?

Dozens of neighborhoods offer nearly every way to live in Southwest Florida: low-rise and mid-rise condominiums with golf and preserve views, coach homes and attached villas, and single-family residences from golf-course cottages to bayfront estates with private docks. Condos and coach homes generally start in the $400,000s, with single-family homes ranging through the high six figures and estates well into the millions.

Within the gates sits The Colony at Pelican Landing — a luxury village with its own high-rise towers overlooking Estero Bay, its own golf and bay club structure, and a private beach experience of its own. It's effectively a community within the community, and choosing between Pelican Landing proper and The Colony is one of the first conversations we have with buyers here.

The Fine Print

What should buyers know about fees and rules?

Pelican Landing runs on a master community association plus neighborhood sub-associations, and the master dues are widely considered among the best values in Southwest Florida for what they include — the beach island and shuttles, tennis, pickleball, fitness, sailing, and the grounds of a 2,365-acre community. Golf at The Nest is a separate, optional membership decision. Sub-association fees vary meaningfully by neighborhood, especially between condos, coach homes, and single-family enclaves.

Rental rules protect the residential character: generally one lease per year with a thirty-day minimum, with neighborhoods able to add their own restrictions. As with every community we serve, we verify the exact three-layer numbers — master, neighborhood, and club — for any specific home before our buyers write an offer.

Common Questions

Pelican Landing, Answered

Does Pelican Landing really have a private beach island?
Yes — and it's unlike anything else in Southwest Florida. Pelican Landing's 34-acre Beach Park sits at the northern end of Big Hickory Island on the Gulf and is reachable only by the community's boat shuttles from the marina on Estero Bay. It's staffed year-round, with an elevated beach house, picnic pavilions, restrooms and showers, and beach chairs and umbrellas provided. No public access, no parking battles — just a shuttle ride to your own island.
Is golf membership required in Pelican Landing?
No. The Nest — Pelican's Nest Golf Club — is a member-owned club inside the community with two Tom Fazio-designed 18-hole championship courses, and membership is optional. That means you can enjoy the beach island, tennis, sailing, and every association amenity without carrying golf dues, or add golf at the level that fits your life.
What amenities come with Pelican Landing's HOA fees?
A remarkable list for the money: the private beach island and shuttle, a tennis center with 12 Har-Tru courts, six pickleball courts, bocce, a community center with a fully equipped fitness center, a sailing center, a kayak and canoe park on Spring Creek, and marina facilities on Estero Bay with a community boat ramp. Golf is the only major amenity that's separate and optional.
Can I keep a boat in Pelican Landing?
Yes. Boat slips can be leased at the community-owned marina on Estero Bay and at the Spring Creek dock, and there's a community boat ramp that gives off-site-stored boats affordable water access. Between the marina, the sailing center, and the kayak park, Pelican Landing is one of the most genuinely boat-friendly communities in Bonita Springs.
What types of homes are in Pelican Landing, and what do they cost?
Roughly 3,300 residences span dozens of neighborhoods: condominiums and coach homes generally starting in the $400,000s, attached villas and single-family homes in the mid ranges, and estate homes on preserves, golf, or water reaching well into the millions. The Colony at Pelican Landing, a luxury village within the community, adds high-rise living with Gulf views and its own club structure.
Can I rent out a home in Pelican Landing?
Rentals are deliberately limited to protect the residential feel: generally one lease per year with a 30-day minimum, and individual neighborhoods can add their own rules. If rental flexibility matters to your plans, tell us early — we'll verify the exact policy for any home before you commit.
