The Quick Answer

What is Spanish Wells?

Spanish Wells is a gated golf community of roughly 600 acres in Bonita Springs, Florida, set at the end of Spanish Wells Boulevard just south of Bonita Beach Road. Its country club dates to 1979 — long before this stretch of Bonita Springs grew into what it is today — and the community around it holds about 1,361 residences across eight distinct neighborhoods, from coach homes and condominiums to estate-sized single-family homes.

What makes Spanish Wells genuinely different in Southwest Florida is flexibility: in seven of its eight neighborhoods, joining the golf club is entirely optional. You can live behind the gates with a full golf membership, a social membership, or no membership at all — a rarity in a region where most golf communities bundle club costs into every home.

The Club

What makes the golf club different here?

Spanish Wells Golf & Country Club plays 27 holes across three 9-hole courses, combined into three different 18-hole routings — so the course you play Tuesday isn't the course you play Friday. The original eighteen came from architect Gordon Lewis; the North nine, designed by Bruce Howard, was added in 1996 as the community grew. Mature oaks, tropical palms, and water throughout give it the feel of an older, settled Florida course rather than a modern housing-tract layout.

The club's modern chapter matters to buyers: in January 2018 the community's homeowners association purchased the golf course itself, putting the residents in control of their own amenity with professional management running daily operations. Beyond golf, the club offers tennis, pickleball, a community pool, a fitness center, and a clubhouse restaurant with a busy social calendar — with membership tiers from full golf down to social, plus options for non-residents.

Location

Where is Spanish Wells, and what's nearby?

Few communities put you closer to everything Bonita Springs does well. Spanish Wells sits a half mile south of Bonita Beach Road, just below the Lee–Collier county line — drive west about fifteen minutes and you're at Bonita Beach and Barefoot Beach Preserve; drive east and you reach I-75 in minutes, with RSW International Airport about half an hour north. Coconut Point, Promenade at Bonita Bay, and the revitalized downtown Bonita Springs river district are all short drives, and everyday errands — groceries, pharmacies, medical offices — line Bonita Beach Road itself.

Homes & Neighborhoods

What kinds of homes are in Spanish Wells?

Eight neighborhoods make up Spanish Wells, and the range is wider than most golf communities offer. Coach homes and condominiums — in enclaves like Las Brisas and Marbella — provide low-maintenance living with community pools, often with lake or fairway views. Single-family neighborhoods run from comfortable golf-course homes to large estate residences with private pools. In today's market, condos and coach homes generally start in the mid-$200,000s, while single-family homes range from around the $500,000s up past $1.5 million for updated estates — making Spanish Wells one of the more attainable gates in coastal Bonita Springs.

Because each neighborhood has its own character, fees, and rules, the smartest first step is telling us how you want to live — golf every day, snowbird simplicity, room for the grandkids — and letting us match you to the right enclave.

The Fine Print

What should buyers know about fees and rules?

Spanish Wells runs on a master community association plus a sub-association for each neighborhood, and there is no CDD. What your dues cover varies meaningfully by enclave — some coach-home neighborhoods include nearly everything except electricity — so comparing two Spanish Wells homes means comparing two fee structures, not just two prices. Club membership is a separate, optional decision in seven of the eight neighborhoods, with tiers from full golf to social.

Renting is allowed but deliberately limited to keep the community residential: leases require at least thirty continuous days, subleasing isn't permitted, and individual neighborhoods can layer on their own rules. We verify the exact fees, membership options, and rental policy for any specific home before you write an offer — it's part of every Spanish Wells consultation we do.

Common Questions

Spanish Wells, Answered

Is golf membership required in Spanish Wells?
No — and that's one of the biggest reasons buyers choose Spanish Wells. Seven of the community's eight neighborhoods have no bundled membership requirement, so you can join the golf club, take a social membership, or simply enjoy the gated community without club dues at all. Very few Southwest Florida golf communities offer that flexibility.
How far is Spanish Wells from the beach?
About 15 minutes. Spanish Wells sits just south of Bonita Beach Road, and a straight drive west takes you to Bonita Beach and Barefoot Beach Preserve — some of the finest Gulf sand in Southwest Florida.
Does Spanish Wells have a CDD?
No. Spanish Wells is an established community with no CDD. Costs run through the master community association and your specific neighborhood's sub-association, which vary by enclave — we walk every buyer through the exact numbers for the neighborhood they're considering.
What amenities does Spanish Wells Golf & Country Club offer?
The club centers on 27 holes of championship golf played in three 9-hole combinations, plus tennis, pickleball, a community pool, a fitness center, and a clubhouse with a restaurant and an active social calendar. Membership tiers range from full golf to social.
Can I rent out a home in Spanish Wells?
Yes, with limits designed to keep the neighborhood residential: leases require at least 30 continuous days and subleasing is not permitted. Individual neighborhood associations may add their own rules, so we verify the exact rental policy for any home you're considering.
What types of homes are in Spanish Wells?
Eight distinct neighborhoods offer a real range: coach homes and condominiums with low-maintenance living, and single-family homes from comfortable golf-course villas up to large estate residences with pools and lake or fairway views. That range makes Spanish Wells one of the more attainable ways into a gated Bonita Springs golf community.
