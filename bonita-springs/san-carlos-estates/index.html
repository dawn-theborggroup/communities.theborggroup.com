San Carlos Estates is one of the most genuinely distinctive communities in Bonita Springs — a neighborhood that sits entirely within city limits yet feels nothing like the manicured gated communities that define most of Southwest Florida. Here, lots run approximately 1.25 acres, the roads are private, and the AG-2 zoning creates a level of personal freedom that simply does not exist in the surrounding area.

The community spans a range of home styles and price points — from classic older Florida-style ranch homes on generous lots to newly constructed estate residences priced at $2 million and above. You are 8 miles from Bonita Beach, minutes from Coconut Point Mall, and 20 minutes from RSW International Airport — with none of the HOA overhead that comes with every other established community in the corridor.

All roads within San Carlos Estates are private, funded by an annual assessment on each property's tax bill. This is not an HOA — there are no community rules, no architectural review, and no rental restrictions. It is simply infrastructure maintenance.

Understanding the Zoning

What AG-2 Zoning Means
for Buyers & Owners

AG-2 is an agricultural residential zoning designation that applies throughout San Carlos Estates — and nowhere else in this part of Bonita Springs. It is the foundation of everything that makes this community different.

Boats, RVs & Trailers

AG-2 zoning and the absence of an HOA mean owners can store boats, RVs, trailers, and equipment directly on their property — no approval required. On a 1.25-acre lot there is room to store a large vessel, a recreational vehicle, and equipment simultaneously. For buyers frustrated by HOA restrictions elsewhere, this is a primary reason San Carlos Estates becomes the destination.

Animals & Agricultural Use

AG-2 zoning permits horses, goats, chickens, and other animals on appropriately sized lots — something categorically unavailable in any gated community in the area. Owners can also use their property for light agricultural or hobby-farm activities consistent with the zoning designation. For buyers wanting to maintain a rural lifestyle while accessing city amenities, San Carlos Estates is genuinely rare in this market.

Build Without Approval Committees

Want to build a detached garage, a workshop, a guest cottage, or a pole barn? In a gated community, each requires HOA architectural review. In San Carlos Estates, you work directly with the City of Bonita Springs permitting office. The lot size supports substantial additional structures, and the zoning accommodates them. New construction activity here has been growing meaningfully for exactly this reason.

What Buyers Need to Know

Private Roads & the Tax Assessment

All roads within San Carlos Estates are private — meaning they are not maintained by the city or Lee County. Instead, road and drainage infrastructure is funded through an annual assessment that appears on each property's tax bill.

This is not an HOA fee. It comes with no community rules, no architectural review process, no rental restrictions, and no association governance whatsoever. Think of it as a utility assessment — you pay for the infrastructure that serves your property, and in return the roads and drainage are maintained. The exact annual amount varies and should be confirmed on the current tax record for any specific property you are considering.

A Significant Financial Advantage

Flood Zone X — No Flood Insurance Required

In a region where flood insurance costs run from several hundred to several thousand dollars per year, San Carlos Estates' Flood Zone X designation is a meaningful financial differentiator. Flood Zone X properties are outside the high-risk flood area. Flood insurance is not required for federally backed mortgages, and for buyers who choose to carry it anyway, premiums are substantially lower than in high-risk zones. This matters both for annual carrying costs and for the eventual resale conversation.

Common Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

What is San Carlos Estates in Bonita Springs? +
San Carlos Estates is a unique acreage community within Bonita Springs city limits, zoned AG-2 (agricultural residential) — the only neighborhood of its kind in this part of the city. Lots typically run approximately 1.25 acres, all roads are private, and there is no HOA and no CDD. Homes range from classic older Florida-style ranch homes to newly constructed estate residences priced at $2 million and above.
Does San Carlos Estates have an HOA? +
No HOA and no CDD. There are no monthly association fees, no architectural review, and no deed restrictions beyond AG-2 zoning and Bonita Springs city code. There is an annual assessment on each property's tax bill that funds maintenance of the private roads and drainage infrastructure — this is not an HOA fee and comes with no community rules or governance.
Is San Carlos Estates in a flood zone? +
San Carlos Estates is designated FEMA Flood Zone X — outside the high-risk flood area. Flood insurance is not required for federally backed mortgages on Flood Zone X properties. This is a meaningful financial advantage over much of coastal Southwest Florida where Flood Zone AE designation and mandatory flood insurance are the norm.
What is AG-2 zoning and what does it allow? +
AG-2 is an agricultural residential zoning designation unique to San Carlos Estates in this part of Bonita Springs. It permits storing boats, RVs, trailers, and equipment on the property; keeping horses, goats, chickens, and other animals; building detached garages, workshops, guest structures, and agricultural buildings; and operating light agricultural or hobby-farm activities — all without HOA approval.
Can I store my boat or RV in San Carlos Estates? +
Yes — this is one of the primary reasons buyers choose San Carlos Estates. AG-2 zoning and the absence of an HOA mean you can store boats, RVs, trailers, and equipment on your property without restriction. On a 1.25-acre lot there is ample room for a large vessel, a recreational vehicle, and equipment simultaneously — something categorically unavailable in any gated community in Bonita Springs or Estero.
What is the road and drainage assessment? +
San Carlos Estates has an annual assessment included in each property's tax bill that funds maintenance of its private roads and drainage system. This is purely an infrastructure fee — not an HOA. It carries no community rules, no architectural review, no rental restrictions, and no governance. The exact amount varies by property and should be confirmed on the current tax record when evaluating any specific home.
How far is San Carlos Estates from the beach? +
San Carlos Estates is approximately 8 miles from Bonita Beach and Barefoot Beach Preserve — roughly a 15-minute drive. The community sits within Bonita Springs city limits, providing access to Coconut Point Mall, the Imperial River dining district, and RSW International Airport (approximately 20 minutes).
Is San Carlos Estates within Bonita Springs city limits? +
Yes. Despite its spacious, semi-rural character, San Carlos Estates is entirely within the city limits of Bonita Springs. Residents receive city services and have full access to the Bonita Springs amenity base — all while enjoying the space and freedom of a large-lot, no-HOA, AG-2-zoned community.
